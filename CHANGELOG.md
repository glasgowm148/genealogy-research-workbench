# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The people catalogue is a **Find a person** page with seven filter menus: **Name** (first names, last name at birth or current, full name, gender, exact spelling), **Relatives** (a row per spouse, father, mother, child or sibling), **Dates** (any record, birth or death, from and to), **Places** (any, any record, birth, marriage, death or biography, a country or region, places to exclude), **Evidence** (the study naming someone, the record types and quality of the records cited, and the claims filter), **Filters** (born or married into the project's surname, a name suffix, missing father or mother, descendants, no spouse) and a tree menu (no external link, pending relationship changes by relationship, action and new confidence, read from findings not yet reviewed or applied). A header links the name index, the downloads and the held and pending research; the result toolbar adds **Family branch** grouping, a **Location detail** level and a **Sort results** menu with best match and relationship-change orders. Every control is a real form control and is kept in the address under its own key; the old `birth_from`, `death_from` and `no_parent` addresses still work, and `?surname=` links show the surname in the Name menu. New optional `[names] surname` and `[site] tree_name` settings word the page, and `data/site-data.js` carries each person's given names, name kinds, studies, cited record types and qualities and pending changes. The **Recorded sex** menu (with its "Not recorded" choice), the separate birth and death year ranges and the surname menu are replaced by these controls. See [Name the surname and the online tree](docs/research.md#name-the-surname-and-the-online-tree).
- `Place.region` names the country or broad region a project files a place under, in its own words; the places page and the catalogue's **Country or region** menu use it, falling back to the last part of the place name as written. When a project has studies with `kind = "group"`, the catalogue's **Possible group** menu lists only those.
- The README shows the site as it now is, with screenshots refreshed by the new `tools/readme_screenshots.py` (which builds the demo, serves it locally and captures each page).
- The findings log and each archive page open with a **Months** list between the legend and the table: a link to each month's row group on that page, newest first, with its count ("October 2026 · 12"); past 24 months the older ones go by year under **Earlier years**. Each month's `<tbody>` has an id (`month-2026-10`, `month-undated`), so a month can be linked to. See [the findings log](docs/deployment.md).
- The person dossier gains **Pending <tree_name> updates** (after **Research**, with an **On this page** entry and a **Pending updates** button in **Explore**) when a finding not yet reviewed or applied changes one of the person's relationships: each change with the other person's role and name, its before → after badge, note, the finding linked and dated and its significance, then **Open on WikiTree ↗** to the person's first tree profile. A relationship change may now carry an optional `significance` (0–100). Withheld findings, links and people are never listed. See [the pages produced](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- Update priority in the catalogue: a relationship change in `Finding.changes` may carry `significance` (0–100, validated). Under a person's name the catalogue shows "Needs updated on <tree_name> · significance N" and a chip per pending change ("father now Strongly supported 90%", "father added", "father replaced"), the same from the page and the script; **Sort results** gains **Update priority: new fathers first**, **Update significance** and **Father changes: highest confidence** (`sort=priority`, `significance`, `father-changes`); and **Table / Map** is a tab list with arrow-key movement. `data/site-data.js` carries each pending change's significance and link estimate. See [Build and inspect](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- A **DNA** page for any project: the new `Haplogroup` record (name, parent branch, date as reported, note) and `Person.haplogroup_id` with `haplogroup_note` (how the person is attached: "reported root", "tested descendant line") model the branch structure only, never test results or raw DNA. When the case has public branches the build writes `dna.html` (titled by the new optional `[site] dna_heading`, default "DNA", under Research in the sidebar) with **The genetic structure** (the branches as nested lists and an SVG chain of links, each with its date, note and people), **Documentary lines** (the public people on each branch and how they are attached) and **Interpretation** (studies with `kind = "dna"`), and a page per branch at `dna/<slug>.html` with its line of descent, people, child branches and studies; each dossier's hero names the person's branch. Validation rejects missing parents and branches and cycles; a branch whose parent is withheld is withheld with it, and a public person on a withheld branch is published without it. See [Record DNA branches](docs/research.md#record-dna-branches).
- The home page gains a second half led by the project's own research, all written by the build with no JavaScript: **Priority studies** (up to eight places numbered by the studies and research notes about them, each listing its studies, on the bundled basemap when they have coordinates), **A question we are investigating** (the highest-priority open question, or `[home] featured_question`, with who it concerns, up to four dated events, what is still open and links to the research), **Featured studies** cards with a number, the `Study.kind` kicker and a people count (the newest three, or `[home] studies`), and lead-in panels: **How are two people related?**, a DNA panel when the site has a `dna.html` page, and **Continue the research** cards from `[home] resources`. The demo sets a featured question and two resources. See [Configure your project](docs/deployment.md#configure-your-project).
- Saved catalogue views: `[[catalogue.views]]` entries in `genealogy.toml`, each a `label` and a catalogue address `query` (such as `no_external=1`), are listed in the sidebar under **People** after **People catalogue** as `catalogue.html?<query>` links, and the catalogue marks the view its address carries as the current page. Labels and queries are checked when the project loads: only the catalogue's own parameters, each once with a value. The demo has two. See [Configure your project](docs/deployment.md#configure-your-project).
- **Families & branches** tells each branch's story: studies of kind `family` (or `family study`) are listed as cards in a new **Family studies** section at the top of the page (type, title, summary, people count and **Read the study →**; the newest 24 on a large site), and each surname and connected group that includes a study's people shows the study naming most of them, with up to three more by title. No JavaScript is needed. See [Write a study](docs/research.md#write-a-study).

The site index opens with **Choose a way in**, six numbered entry tiles, and its downloads are a table of each file's format, size and contents, placed before the person index. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md).

The recorded value stays in a `<time datetime>` or `data-date` attribute, and `data/site-data.js` carries a precision only when it changes a printed date. Family links read the right way round ("Ada Fable is the mother of Cara Fable"), connection steps say who is whose child, parent or spouse, references name places, events and claims instead of printing their IDs, the dossier and place pages show "Record ID: …" as small text, connected groups are named "Group of N people around <earliest person>", short lists are pills, and counts are worded with one plural rule ("1 evidence assessment", "1 of 1 study shown"). See [Date precision](docs/research.md#date-precision).

Each place page lists its events as a Year · Event · Person · Source table that sorts by year. Past 100 places the places page shows the busiest and each region's pages hold the rest. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md#large-sites).
- The sidebar groups the pages into six sections with icons — People, Family, Places, Records, Research and About — after **Home**; the current section lists its pages with the current one marked, and **Find a connection →** and **Site map** sit below. A new top bar carries a breadcrumb (Home / People / Ada Fable), a build stamp ("Catalogue · 27 Sep 2026", from the newest public finding or study update, never the clock) and the site search, which ⌘K, Ctrl+K or `/` now reach and whose results open over the page instead of pushing it down. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- Two new pages, **About the data** (`about-data.html`) and **Change log** (`changes.html`), and described downloads. About the data gives the public counts of people, events, places and sources, dated by the newest finding or study update rather than the build clock; explains **Why the page totals differ** between the catalogue, the family tree, the map and the findings, a line each; draws every badge family from **Reading the evidence** and the solid and dashed lines of the tree views; and notes what the site withholds, with buttons to the downloads. The change log lays out a dated card per `data/progress-history.json` snapshot ("+1 person, +1 assessed link since 2 Oct 2026") and per month of findings, with **Looking for new facts? Latest findings →**. The site index's downloads table gives each file's format, contents and size as built. See [the pages produced](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- **Research** leads with the open work: a count of open questions, open hypotheses, draft findings and searches logged, linked to each section; open questions as cards, high priority first, with the people, study, plan and linked evidence; then hypotheses (competing ones still side by side), findings in a compact list, and the claims. Up to 300 claims are no longer repeated as full cards: each is one line in a collapsible list per person, linked to its full card on the dossier, and keeps its anchor (and its assessments' anchors) on **Research** so existing links still land. See [Record a research question](docs/research.md#record-a-research-question).
- Study pages are structured: a **← Studies** link, a type label from the new optional `Study.kind`, a strip of counts (people, linked records, events, findings linked), a two-column header with the summary and rationale beside a locator map marking each located place of the study's people and linked places (left out when none has coordinates), a **What this does not show** callout from the new optional `Study.limitations`, then the conclusion and the linked people and records grouped by type (People, Places, Sources, Findings, Questions, …) with counts. The conclusion now follows the chronology and groups, and **Related findings and questions** is folded into the grouped list. See [Write a study](docs/research.md#write-a-study).
- The person dossier opens with a hero card: name, lifespan, a **Born**/**Died** box with each place linked, the generated opening sentences, action buttons (Family tree, Find a connection, Compare, the surname filter, Suggest a correction and the save control) and the **Latest finding** beside them; on a phone it stacks. The **On this page** menu is numbered and, on screens 1100px and wider, a column kept in view beside the sections. A new **Connected pages** section at the end groups the person's places, studies, sources cited, surname filter and parents' and children's dossiers, with counts, the first 12 of each, and nothing from a withheld record. See [Build and inspect](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- A **Records** page (`records.html`): one row per public citation with its year, the people whose claims cite it (three, then a count), record type, place, strongest assessment (or "Not yet assessed") and the source and locator linked to the citation with the start of its abstract, under a count and a strip of how many are assessed, unassessed, abstracted and quality-stated. With JavaScript it searches, filters by record type, year range and assessment, sorts and pages every citation from a new `data/records-index.js`, keeping the view in the address; past 100 citations `records/page-<n>.html` holds the rest. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md#large-sites).
- One badge system: every confidence, grade, status, record-quality, finding-type and change pill (in the pages and those the script draws) is a link to its entry in **Reading the evidence**, coloured by its family and marked with a small glyph (✓ ~ ? ○ ✕ ◇) beside the words; a tree's own mark is now a badge of its own. The guide opens with **The labels at a glance**, drawing each badge once with its one-line meaning, and gains an entry for finding types.
- Large sites now page sources 80 to a page (was 100), so a page of full source cards stays under the 100 KB budget now that every type badge carries its family.
- The findings log is a table: Date, Finding, People, Records, and Status and kind, stacked into labelled cards on narrow screens, with a count, a legend of the status and type badges, a link to the Atom feed (with `site.base_url`), and **Findings by study** and **Findings by place** counts linking to `findings.html?study=<id>` and `?place=<id>`. With JavaScript, filters for words, type, status, person, study and place, plus order, are kept in the address; a log with archive pages writes `data/findings-index.js` so the filters cover every finding. Archive pages use the same table. "1 evidence assessments" now reads "1 evidence assessment". See [Record a finding](docs/research.md#record-a-finding).
- Compare and Connections are one-line forms: a type-to-find box per side (the menus stay in the form for readers without JavaScript), a ⇄ swap button, a **Compare** / **Find the connection →** button, example placeholders, and **Start with a known question** pairs (people sharing a surname; the longest recorded paths). Connections adds an evidence threshold and options for partnerships and uncertain links, and reads as a sentence per step with a summary ("… is a great-grandchild of …") and a link to each link's evidence. Compare marks shared values and links places and relatives. Both link **Browse the name index →** instead of listing every person. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- The people catalogue is a research table: each person's birth and death (date in words, place), spouses, father and mother, every relative linked with the dossier's badge for that link and rejected links left out, the first external identifier in place of the record ID where there is one, and cells shaded by date certainty and by whether the links are documented or probable. Column headings sort the table; the filters are grouped into **Name**, **Relatives**, **Dates**, **Places**, **Evidence** and **Filters** pills that count what is on; **Group** adds heading rows by birth region or surname; and **Table / Map** shows the matching people's birth places on the offline basemap with a text list. The page shows 50 people at a time (and a large site writes 50 per catalogue page), the **Sort by** menu is replaced by the headings, and the address keeps the new `sort` values, `group_location`, `group_surname` and `view`. `data/site-data.js` carries each person's first external identifier. See [Build and inspect](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).

## 0.7.0 — 21 October 2026

- Old person links keep working after a merge or an ID change: list the IDs a person replaced in the new `Person.former_ids`, and the site writes a small page at each old address that sends readers to the current dossier (marked `noindex`, with a canonical link when `site.base_url` is set), plus `data/redirects.json` and, with `site.base_url`, a `_redirects` file of 301 rules for hosts that read one. Moved pages stay out of `data/routes.json` and `sitemap.xml`, a rebuild removes those no longer needed, and a withheld person's former IDs produce nothing. Validation rejects a former ID that is a current record ID or is listed by two people, and a reference that uses a former ID names the ID to use instead. `import --update` keeps the former IDs recorded on an imported person, and `check-links` now checks `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` targets. See [Record a merge or a changed ID](docs/research.md#record-a-merge-or-a-changed-id).
- A recorded sibling link (`kind: "sibling"`, `half_sibling` and similar) now appears under **Siblings** on both dossiers with its own label, merged with any sibling found through shared parents, and the tree's family view draws it. A disproved link is listed only under **All recorded relationships**; the GEDCOM export still has no sibling record. See [Assess a relationship](docs/research.md#assess-a-relationship).
- The studies page can be searched by title, summary or the people a study links. Past 300 studies it writes the 100 most recently updated, `studies/page-<n>.html` lists every study by title, and the search covers every study from `data/site-data.js`, which carries no study bodies. The site index links to these pages instead of listing every study. See [Large sites](docs/deployment.md#large-sites).
- A research question can record what evidence would answer it (`resolves`) and the next record to check (`next_record`). An open question shows both on **Research** and in each related dossier, with "Not yet identified" for a blank. See [Record a research question](docs/research.md#record-a-research-question).
- A corrections page: set `contact_email` or an https `contact_url` under `[site]` (with an optional `corrections_note`) and the site gains `corrections.html`, explaining what a useful correction contains and asking for no details of living people, plus a footer link and a **Suggest a correction** link on every person's page that starts an email with the person's name and record ID. Without a contact there is no page and no link, and a rebuild removes them. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md#configure-your-project).
- Each claim shows its records as one table: the record, what it says (its abstract or quotation), the assessment and the assessment's new optional `limitation`. A cited record nobody has assessed yet shows "Not yet assessed". See [Summarise a record](docs/research.md#summarise-a-record).
- Spellings of one surname can be grouped under `[names.variants]` in `genealogy.toml`, for example `Fable = ["Fabel", "Faible"]`. **Families & branches** shows one row for the group with the spellings recorded ("Recorded as Fabel (3), Faible (1)"), the catalogue's surname filter covers the whole group, and a catalogue search for any spelling finds the others. Names stay as recorded on dossiers and in the downloads. See [Group spellings of a surname](docs/research.md#group-spellings-of-a-surname).
- Each source on the sources page lists what it is cited for: the public people whose claims rest on its citations (with the claims, for example "Ada Fable — birth, occupation"), the findings citing it and the family links whose claims cite it, the first 20 of each and then a count. The sources page adds a **By record type** table (sources, citations and people cited per `source_type`) and groups the sources by type; past 300 sources, each type has its own pages, `sources/type-<type>.html`. `[source_types.<key>]` in `genealogy.toml` gives a type a `label` and a reader's caution (`note`). See [Explain a record type](docs/research.md#explain-a-record-type).
- A finding can record which relationship or claim it reassessed, and from what (`Finding.changes`, `finding add --change RECORD_ID:BEFORE:AFTER`). The family row or claim reads "Changed from Possible to Documented, 2026-09-08", linked to the finding, and the findings log, research notebook and Atom feed show "Possible → Documented". Validation checks that each value is a confidence or a defined grade and that a record's latest change ends at its current grade or confidence. A finding that changed a withheld record is withheld. See [Record a finding](docs/research.md#record-a-finding).
- Each dossier opens with an **On this page** menu linking only the sections that have something in them, held at the top of the window on screens 1450px and wider, and an **Explore** line: the family tree focused on the person, **Find a connection** and **Compare** with the person already chosen, and the catalogue filtered to their surname. Compare and Connections read `?a=<id>&b=<id>`, show the result when both are given and move to the other menu when only one is. Each dossier section heading has a stable id (`#events`, `#claims`, …) for linking.
- Each family link on a dossier says why it is shown: the relationship's new optional `rationale`, else its first linked claim's, and "Supporting records" linking the citations its claims cite (five, then a count). A disproved link, or one graded as disproved, moves from Parents, Spouses and partners, Children or Siblings to **Rejected and superseded links** at the end of the family section, with its badge and reason, and no longer triggers the competing-parents notice. A dossier without notes opens with plain sentences built from public events and current links ("Ada Fable was born ABT 1801 in Invented Parish, and died 12 MAR 1870 in Invented Town. This page lists …"), and every dossier with a dated public finding shows a **Latest finding** box with "Research last recorded <date>". See [Assess a relationship](docs/research.md#assess-a-relationship).
- A citation can record its `quality` (`original`, `derivative` or `authored`) and its `information` (`primary`, `secondary` or `undetermined`), in the standard *Evidence Explained* terms. The source page and each claim's evidence table show them as "Original record · primary information", linked to new **Record quality** entries in **Reading the evidence**, and the catalogue's evidence filter offers **Backed by an original record**. `data/site-data.js` carries claims' citation ids and the citations that state either term. See [Summarise a record](docs/research.md#summarise-a-record).
- Each study page draws a chronology of its people: one line per person in `related_ids`, ordered by first dated event, from a recorded birth to a recorded death (otherwise first to last dated event), with a marker per public event coloured by kind and the event's date, kind and place on hover. Approximate dates are drawn at their year; undated events are counted under the drawing; a study with fewer than two dated events has none, and past 30 people the rest are counted, not drawn. It is a static SVG with a text version, so it needs no JavaScript. See [Write a study](docs/research.md#write-a-study).
- A study can record the households or groups it compares (`Study.groups`: id, title, member people, note), how they connect (`Study.links`: from and to groups, a confidence value or grade, a note and Evidence or Citation records) and the records still to inspect (`Study.next_records`). The study page lists each group with its linked members and their years under a caution that membership does not by itself assert kinship, draws the groups and their links as a static SVG (lines labelled with the status, dashed unless documented or probable, up to 20 groups) with a text version, and lists the records still to inspect. A grouped person's dossier links to the study, and a study grouping a withheld person or citing withheld evidence for a link is withheld. See [Compare households and groups](docs/research.md#compare-households-and-groups).
- A research log: the new `Search` record keeps each search made — where you looked (`search`), what was found, including nothing (`result`), when (`searched_on`), what it could not cover (`limitation`), the people, places or sources it was for (`related_ids`) and any records found (`citation_ids`). `genealogy search add ID "TEXT" --result "..." --for ID… [--on DATE] [--cite ID…] [--limitation "..."]` adds one; `genealogy search TEXT` still searches the case. Each dossier gains a **Research log** section after **Research**, newest first, linked from the **On this page** menu, and `research.html` ends with the newest 50 searches and the total. A search naming a withheld person, place, source or citation is withheld, and searches are in `data/site.json` and `data/site.csv`. See [Log a search](docs/research.md#log-a-search).
- A page for every place with a public event or a place within it (`places/pl-<hash>.html`): the places it lies within, from `Place.parent_id`, the places within it, its coordinates with a small marker map, the people recorded there with their events in date order, the surnames recorded there, the years recorded and the studies that list or mention it. Event lists, dossiers and studies now link to these pages; a place without one keeps its entry on the places page. See [Place hierarchy](docs/research.md#place-hierarchy).
- **Reading the evidence** says what each grade usually rests on ("Usually rests on: a record that names both people and the relationship between them, …" for Documented, and similar for the other standard grades) and shows one example from the site under each confidence, grade and claim status entry: the first public family link with it, such as "Example: Ada Fable and her father Bram Fable" linked to Ada's family section, or the first public claim. A project grade can state its own with an optional `basis` under `[grades.<key>]`. See [Use your own grades](docs/research.md#use-your-own-grades).
- The catalogue has **Research filters**: recorded sex, no parent recorded, has recorded children, no recorded spouse or partner, birth and death year ranges (beside the existing event-year range) and an event place, on public records only, for the written-out catalogue and a large site's catalogue built from the data alike. Every search, filter and sort now lives in the page address (`catalogue.html?surname=Fable&sex=F&birth_from=1800&no_parent=1`), so a filtered view can be bookmarked or shared, and **Clear search and filters** resets them. `data/site-data.js` gives each person with a current sex claim a short `sex` value (`F`, `M` or the value as recorded). See [Build and inspect](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- A **Statistics** page (`statistics.html`) counts the public record: births by century and by country or region, the most common given names and surnames, the largest families, the most recorded places, moves from birth to death region, events by kind, and research progress (claims by status, family links by assessment, findings by year). Each is a plain table with a bar per row, needs no JavaScript, lists at most 25 rows, and is left out when there is nothing to count; withheld people and records are never counted. See [deployment](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- A hypothesis can record the points for it (`supports`) and against it (`objections`), each a list of plain sentences. When two or more open hypotheses concern the same people (the same `related_ids`), **Research** and each of those people's dossiers set them side by side as cards: the statement, its label, "For" and "Against" as recorded, and its evidence assessments, in id order. A lone, rejected or disproved hypothesis shows its points in its own entry. Nothing is counted, scored or ranked, and the site never picks one; **Reading the evidence** explains that For and Against are the researcher's recorded points, not a tally. See [Record a hypothesis](docs/research.md#record-a-hypothesis).
- A **Research progress** page (`progress.html`) shows how far the research has got: the share of people with an assessed parent link, parent links by grade in the project's own wording, each connected group's strongest and weakest link and the groups by weakest link, family heads (people with descendants but no recorded parent) by number of descendants, open questions and draft findings, and the newest 20 findings that changed a grade. `data/progress-history.json` keeps a snapshot per newest-finding date (the latest 60), read back from the output folder on each build and dated from the findings, never the clock, so a rebuild of the same research is identical; the page draws the assessed-parent share over the snapshots as a static line with a table. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md#build-and-inspect).
- An optional **Possible duplicates** page (`duplicates.html`), switched on with `[research] duplicates = true` in `genealogy.toml`: pairs of public people with the same surname (counting `[names.variants]` spellings as one) and first given name, ignoring case and accents, born within two years of each other or, without a birth year, sharing an event place, and not already linked by any recorded relationship. Each pair shows both names, lifespans and record IDs, the places in common and a **Compare** link. The page presents them as leads for a researcher to check, never as merge suggestions ("Two records may describe one person, or two people who shared a name. Nothing here is a conclusion."). Pairs are found by name group rather than by comparing everyone, listed by surname and name, 100 a page and at most 500, with a count of any beyond. See [Build and deployment](docs/deployment.md#configure-your-project).
- The places map can be filtered by event year range, event type, surname (spelling variants count under their canonical surname) and place name. A place stays on the map while one of its events matches, and a count says how many places and events are shown. Places that would sit within about 12 pixels of each other merge into one marker showing how many it holds, with their names in its tooltip; zooming in splits them, and selecting one lists its places with links to where each is listed. `data/site-data.js` carries those links as `site.place_urls`.
- One search box at the top of every page's sidebar finds people, places, studies, sources, findings and research questions by title. It loads a small index, `data/search-index.json` (and `data/search-index.js`, which also works from `file://`), the first time it is used: a title and one short line per public record (a lifespan, an updated or recorded date, a record type or a question's status), never notes, summaries or bodies. The 12 best matches are grouped by kind and can be reached with the arrow keys, Enter opens one and Escape closes the list; without JavaScript, or with no match, the box searches the people catalogue. ## 0.3.0 — 20 October 2026

Import
- GEDCOM follows its `HEAD.CHAR` declaration: UTF-8/UTF-16 with or without BOM, ANSI (Windows-1252), ANSEL, ASCII, MACINTOSH and ISO-8859; undecodable bytes are reported by line.
- Raw line breaks inside long notes (as written by WikiTree's GEDCOM export) are kept as continued text and reported instead of rejecting the file.
- Parent links keep their supplied nature (`PEDI`, `_FREL`/`_MREL`, `ADOP`, WikiTree non-biological) in `Relationship.pedigree`; the tree's own marks (WikiTree uncertain/confident/DNA confirmed, GEDCOM 7 `STAT`) in `Relationship.tree_status`, never raising confidence. `DISPROVEN` links are disproved and not published.
- Names keep `given`/`surname` parts, nicknames, married and other names; GEDCOM identifiers (`_UID`, `REFN`, `EXID`, `_FSFTID`, `AFN`, `WWW`), source `AUTH`/`PUBL`/call numbers, citation `QUAY`, GEDCOM 7 `SNOTE` and about 40 event types are imported. Unsupported structures are counted in one warning; `SSN`/`IDNO` are never imported.
- Burial or cremation records mark a person deceased. Invalid coordinates are reported and dropped rather than failing the import.
- OneTree: WikiTree `YYYY-00-00` dates and DataStatus guess/before/after qualifiers, `MiddleName`, `LastNameOther`, `Nicknames`, `IsLiving`, one relationship per couple, marriage events from spouse rows, hidden spouses kept private, one summary warning for relatives outside the export.
- `import --update` refreshes a previous import of the same tree and keeps research added to the case, refusing without changes if that research would be orphaned. `--update` and `--replace` first back up the previous case privately.

Publication
- An approximate birth (`ABT`/`EST`/`CAL`) is treated as up to ten years later for the 120-year rule; open-ended dates still need review. `genealogy.publication.is_historical_birth` exposes the rule.
- The recorded sex of eligible people is published as an unassessed tree assertion.

Website
- Dossiers have a family section (parents, partners and marriages, children, siblings) with roles, labels and a competing-parent-records notice.
- One labelled evidence vocabulary (`genealogy.labels`) and a reader's guide, `evidence.html`.
- Interactive pages share one `data/site-data.js` instead of embedding the dataset in every page (works from `file://`).
- Surname filter and column in the catalogue; outstanding research listed first.
- Public GEDCOM 5.5.1 download (`data/public-tree.ged`, `export gedcom`), and `sitemap.xml`/canonical links when `site.base_url` is set.

Research and website (continued)
- `Study` records: a written study with summary, rationale, a small escaped Markdown body with `[[record]]` references, a conclusion and linked subjects. Each study gets a page and an index entry, and dossiers link to it. A study is published only when every record it lists or references is public.
- `Finding.recorded_on` and `finding add --recorded-on` date findings. A **Latest findings** log lists them newest first, with an Atom feed when `site.base_url` is set.
- **Families & branches** page: surname groups with years, places and earliest people, plus connected groups of recorded relatives. The catalogue accepts `?surname=`/`?q=` and the tree accepts `?person=`.
- `Place.precision` (exact … country, or approximate). Broad locations are drawn as dashed rings and labelled on the map and place list.
- `genealogy check-links` and `genealogy.sitecheck`: a static-site link and anchor checker with a ratcheting baseline, extracted from the Glasgow project's release gate.

- Places are grouped by their broadest recorded jurisdiction ("…, Scotland"), with a country or region summary of places, coordinates, events and people. Nothing is looked up.
- `Relationship.estimate`: an optional assessed percentage (1–99), shown as "Probable (70%)". Imports never set it.
- Muted and eyebrow text colours now meet WCAG AA contrast. An opt-in axe-core browser check (`GENEALOGY_AXE_PATH`, run in CI) covers every page type at desktop and mobile widths.

Fixes
- The fictional demo stored parent links from parent to child, contrary to the documented child → parent direction, so its tree drew descendants as ancestors. It is regenerated.
- Browser tests no longer assume a macOS Chrome path (`GENEALOGY_BROWSER_EXECUTABLE`).
- `genealogy.dates` adds `year_of` and `wikitree_date`; sorting understands Julian/dual years, `circa`/`c.`, interpreted dates and month names.

Compatibility: cases gain optional fields (`Name.given/surname`, `Relationship.pedigree/tree_status`, `Source.author/publication`, `Finding.recorded_on`, `Place.precision`, `Relationship.estimate`) and a `Study` record type. Older 0.2 readers cannot load cases written with them. GEDCOM-imported people now record `living` as `"deceased"` rather than `false`. OneTree spouse relationship IDs are now one per couple, so re-import with `--replace` (not `--update`) if research refers to the old per-profile spouse IDs.
